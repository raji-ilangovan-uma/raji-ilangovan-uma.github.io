After the Person Who Always Knew

The annoying thing about living in a world where not everyone knows what you are thinking, and therefore you have to ask for what you need, means that the burden of care falls on yourself. Asking things for yourself is an important skill—don't get me wrong—but when the world is drowning around you and you can barely get a whisper out, sometimes all you can do is flail your arms. Folks could easily say you didn't ask for help, because you didn't shout for it. It feels like that a lot lately. I know I need help, but I don't know how to translate my needs into words.

That's when I think of her more. Because she would know. She always knew. And she always showed up. Sometimes even when I begged her not to. Annoyingly and stubbornly still there. Every time I left, she would stand at the balcony until she couldn't see me any longer. When I returned late, I always found her on that balcony looking out for me. Every airport drop off, she would stand there until I went up the escalator and wouldn't leave until I was at the gate. Every airport pick up, she would be there when the flight landed, ready for me, waiting for me. Having someone stand by you, love you, and hold you, even when you are running away from it all, is something I have lost forever.

Now, when I need her most, she isn't here. Now, I will have to ask to be loved. I will need to find all the ways in which she loved me, write it down as an instructional manual, and hand it to people, knowing full well they will not—cannot—love me like that. When I am drowning, I will need to find a way to float first so I can shout for help, because the hand that would come grab me, is gone.
