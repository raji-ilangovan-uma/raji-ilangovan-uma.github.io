For a Second Time

I have walked this earth searching for her.
In strangers.
In mirrors.
In the ways I laugh.
In the ways I leave.

Again and again,
I have convinced myself,
the way everyone else seems to,
that I am my mother's daughter,
that she lives on in me.

But grief is greedy.

It asks questions
that no one can answer.
What happens after death?

Everyone says she is there,
somewhere,
watching over me.

But I do not see her.
I do not hear her.
I do not feel her hand
reaching back.

And sometimes I wonder,
what if that was it?
What if all I got
was twenty-nine years?

Twenty-nine years of phone calls,
airport pick-ups,
balcony vigils,
ordinary Tuesdays.

Twenty-nine years,
and then silence.

There are moments
when I want to believe
that somewhere beyond this life
she is waiting for me.

But another thought follows close behind:
What if there is nothing?
What if death
does not bring me back to her?
What if it takes her from me again?

And so I stay.

Not because I know
what comes after.

But because this life
is the last place
where I can still find her.

In my memory.
In my stories.
In the shape of the love
she left behind.

If there is nothing after this,
then every day I remain here
is one more day
I get to keep her.

One more day
before I have to lose her
for a second time.
